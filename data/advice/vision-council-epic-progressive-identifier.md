# Vision Council EPIC — what this source actually is

## What this source actually is

Unlike the other documents in this corpus, EPIC is not a guidance document
with a recommended threshold — it's a lens-identification reference: given
a lens's etched manufacturer marking, look up which product it is and what
that product's specifications are. Worth stating plainly so this isn't
cited for something it doesn't claim.

> "Each lens in this catalog is labeled with a sequential number (Item
> Number)... In each index, symbols and lens characteristics are listed
> along with their relevant lens item numbers."
> — EPIC, "How to Use This Catalog"

## Sample entries (real, as fetched — not representative of the full 3,326-page catalog, which was not read exhaustively)

> "Essilor of America — Varilux Physio Enhanced eyecode
> Materials: Plastic (CR39 and 1.50), Trivex, Polycarbonate, Plastic High
> Index (1.60), Plastic High Index (1.66–1.67), Plastic Ultra High Index
> (>1.67)
> Standard Progressive, PAL Design on Front and Back side
> Fitting cross location: 4mm above 180 line
> Rec. minimum fitting height: 14mm"

> "Nikon Optical USA — Presio i Digital
> ... Fitting cross location: 2mm above 180 line
> Rec. minimum fitting height: 18mm"

> "Signet Armorlite, Inc. — Crossbows Custom U
> ... Fitting cross location: 4mm above 180 line
> Rec. minimum fitting height: 13mm"

Sampled entries across three different manufacturers (Essilor, Nikon,
Signet Armorlite) span recommended minimum fitting heights of 13mm to
18mm, consistent with the 14–21mm range found in the Rodenstock, Zeiss,
and HOYA documents in this corpus.

## Application to this project

Cross-manufacturer confirmation, from a fourth independent source, that
**fitting height** (not frame/B-height) sits in the low-to-high teens for
most standard progressive designs. Does not provide a frame/B-height
figure — see `rodenstock-frame-fitting-heights.md` and
`opticampus-progressive-lens-dispensing.md` for those.
